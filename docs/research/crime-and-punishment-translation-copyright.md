# Crime and Punishment English translations — US copyright status

Facts for [Public-domain status of English translations](https://github.com/efarr/story-bearings/issues/4). Not a product choice. Not legal advice: published dates and the rules those dates fall under.

## Question

Which widely used English translations of *Crime and Punishment* are in the public domain in the US, and which are still in copyright? Distinguish the original Russian novel from each translation.

## Two separate works

A translation is a **derivative work**. US law names “a translation” as an example of a derivative work (17 U.S.C. § 101, Copyright Office *Circular 92* / Title 17: <https://www.copyright.gov/title17/title17.pdf>; Compendium glossary: <https://copyright.gov/comp3/docs/glossary.pdf>).

Copyright in a derivative work “extends only to the material contributed by the author of such work, as distinguished from the preexisting material,” and “is independent of, and does not affect or enlarge” copyright in the preexisting material (17 U.S.C. § 103(b): <https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/103>). The Copyright Office’s *Circular 14* states the same rule and lists translations among common derivative works (<https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ14.pdf>).

So: the Russian novel can be public domain while a given English translation remains in copyright, and a public-domain translation does not put a later translation into the public domain.

## US public-domain cutoff in 2026

Cornell’s *Copyright Term and the Public Domain in the United States*, current as of **1 January 2026** (<https://copyright.cornell.edu/publicdomain>; also linked from <https://guides.library.cornell.edu/copyright/publicdomain>):

- Works **first published before 1931**: in the public domain in the US due to copyright expiration. No further conditions.
- Works **first published 1931–1977**: 95 years from publication if formalities were met (notice; renewal for 1931–1963).
- Works **first published 1978 or later**: generally **life of the author + 70 years** (or 95 years from publication / 120 years from creation for corporate authorship).

## Original Russian novel

Project Gutenberg eBook 2554 describes *Crime and Punishment* as a novel **published in 1866** (<https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2554>). 1866 is before 1931. Under the Cornell chart, the original published novel is in the US public domain.

## Translations

### In the US public domain (publication before 1931)

**Constance Garnett (1914).** First printing: London, William Heinemann, 1914, *Crime and punishment: a novel in six parts and an epilogue*, “from the Russian by Constance Garnett” (Niigata University OPAC: <http://opac.lib.niigata-u.ac.jp/en/node/654893>). 1914 is before 1931 → US public domain under the Cornell “before 1931” row.

Project Gutenberg eBook 2554 is that Garnett translation (translator field: Garnett, Constance, 1861–1946) and is marked **“Copyright: Public domain in the USA”** (<https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2554>).

**Frederick Whishaw (1886)** is widely described as the first English translation (Vizetelly, London). That date was not independently confirmed from a library catalog in this pass; if 1886 is accurate it is also before 1931 and would be US public domain. Treat as unverified here until a catalog record is attached.

### Mid-century translations (not fully dated here)

Jessie Coulson (often Oxford, 1950s) and David Magarshack (often Penguin, 1950s) fall in the **1931–1977** band if those years are right: 95 years from publication *if* US notice/renewal rules were satisfied — i.e. still in copyright through the 2040s, not a 2026 public-domain question. First-publication years were not taken from a library catalog in this pass; do not treat them as verified.

Michael Katz (2017) and Nicolas Pasternak Slater (2017) would be life + 70 if those years hold; not verified here from a publisher/catalog primary page.

## What this does and does not decide

- The **1866 Russian text** is US public domain.
- The **Garnett 1914 English text** is US public domain (and is the Gutenberg 2554 text).
- **McDuff (1991), Pevear & Volokhonsky (1992), and Ready (2014)** are US copyrighted translations as of 2026.
- Quoting, which edition Story Bearings follows, and whether original summary prose needs a licensed text are **product decisions**, not settled by these dates.
